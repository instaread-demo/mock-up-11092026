UW–Madison breaks ground on $62 million cyclotron expansion to boost medical isotope production

by Gus Iversen, Editor in Chief | November 04, 2026

The University of Wisconsin School of Medicine and Public Health has broken ground on a $62 million cyclotron expansion that will increase medical radioisotope production and support research into targeted cancer therapies and advanced diagnostic imaging.

The 13,000-square-foot site will add two cyclotrons, one billed as the nation’s first modern alpha-particle machine of its kind, and should be done by May 2028. Output should grow two- to eightfold by isotope, funded by an NIH grant, the Wisconsin Alumni Research Foundation and private gifts.

New products will include astatine-211, a rare alpha emitter under study for cancer care, and theranostics research pairing imaging with targeted radioactive drugs will expand. More supply will also aid PET and SPECT studies of Alzheimer’s, Parkinson’s, heart conditions and other diseases.

UW Health expects more theranostic trials and wider national shipments; the school has supplied isotopes for over a decade through the Department of Energy University Isotope Network.

Jonathan Engle, director of the UW–Madison Cyclotron Research Group, said a national isotope shortage is holding researchers back. The site will also train students in radiochemistry and related fields and support biotech partnerships.
