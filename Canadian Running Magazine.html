Maximilien Filion and Caroline Ash named Under Armour Athletes of the Week

The Thunderbird and X-Woman both capped off undefeated conference seasons and now have their eyes on the U Sports Cross Country Championships

Hayden WestPublished November 6, 2026

The U Sports cross-country championship season is coming to a close, and Canadian Running and Under Armour have teamed up all year to select one male and one female athlete as the Under Armour Athletes of the Week, leading up to the 2026 U Sports Cross Country Championships in Hamilton on Nov. 14. These athletes will be featured on Canadian Running and awarded a shoe from Under Armour’s top performance running lineup in the Velociti family.

This week, we have selected Maximilien Filion of the University of British Columbia and Caroline Ash of St. Francis Xavier University for their respective conference domination. Both athletes remain undefeated as they head into the national championship race in less than two weeks.

Caroline Ash, StFX X-Women (AUS)

In 2024, Ash finished third at the AUS Championships, and in 2025, she finished second. In 2026, she claimed gold, completing an undefeated conference season. At the championship race on Oct. 31, Ash entered with momentum and was ready to take on the stacked competition. StFX hosted the championship race in Antigonish, N.S., and Ash said the community came out to support the squad. With a cheering crowd behind her, she won, in 28:11, 13 seconds ahead of second-place Grace Mack of the Memorial Sea-Hawks. And the secret to Ash’s consistent development? The slow build.

“I’m not a huge fan of running high mileage, but over the years I have been building it slowly and consistently and just trying to not get ahead of myself,” Ash told Canadian Running. “It’s been a long process getting to this point. It was even more special to win at home, which is also where my first race with StFX was and then to finish AUS here felt really good.”

Although Ash said she isn’t into high mileage, she still trains at an average of 90 kilometres a week. While she has been dominating the women’s individual title, her teammate Lalem Sintayehu is undefeated on the men’s side. She said their friendly competition has made the season less stressful.

“It’s been fun! We have been bugging each other about whether we are going to win or not,” Ash said. “He’d ask me before races (women run first in U Sports), and then afterwards I would tell him, now you have to. So, we kept it lighthearted through invitationals, and of course AUS was more stressful, but keeping the momentum from the season was great.”

The two may have kept it breezy as they dominated the AUS circuit, but with U Sports on the horizon, the competition will only get better, and the pressure will build. Despite this, the soft-spoken star remains humble and is excited to keep improving and finish the season strong as she seeks to better her eighth-place finish in 2025.

“Individually, I am always just trying to beat my own score from previous years, and whatever happens on the day happens,” Ash said. “For our team, I just want to see the girls go out there and give it their all. The team culture has been a huge part of my success, and I’ve gained confidence from my coaches and teammates. Headed into U Sports, I think we are capable of more.”

Maximilien Filion, UBC Thunderbirds (CANWES)

Also on Oct. 31, but in Calgary at the Canada West Championships, Fillion completed his undefeated season after a silver medal at the 2025 edition. Championship races were on his mind all year, and he used invitational races to test fitness and competition. Despite almost 1,000 metres of elevation difference (Calgary sits at 1,045 metres above sea level and Vancouver is 72 metres), he was not going to be denied.

“The invitationals were great for testing fitness and making sure legs were there before CANWES,” Filion told Canadian Running. “The taper went great, and we showed up to the race prepared. At the end of it, everything came together, and I felt great even though the altitude was really high.”

Like Ash, Filion’s consistency and build have helped him keep improving. But it isn’t just the physical build that’s helped; his change in mentality also helped him dominate this season. Although he earned a silver medal at CANWES in 2025, Filion said he was disappointed with his U Sports performance after failing to improve on his 17th-place finish in 2024. In 2025, he placed 20th and said the pieces of the puzzle didn’t quite come together. Since 2025, Filion’s consistency and build have helped him keep improving. But it isn’t just the physical build that’s helped; his change in mentality also helped him dominate this season.

“I was really nervous going into the race last year, and this year I’m approaching racing differently,” Filion said. “I’m approaching races with a lot more fun. I’m just happy to be there and excited to compete at the national level, which is always a pleasure. I feel fit, and I’m in a good place mentally, and obviously lots of people target the podium, so for me it would just be the perfect day to get there.”

Although Filion’s individual goal is the podium, he is also excited to see how the team can do this year. He said that if they can improve on their 12th-place finish last year, it would make the day even better, with a top-10 finish as their goal to build momentum for 2027.

Though Filion’s focus is on the upcoming nationals race, he said the support he received along the way is the only reason he’s come so far.

“I just want to give thanks to my girlfriend, family, physio at UBC, my coaches and teammates, and all the people that support me,” Filion said. “I wouldn’t be here without that support, and I want to mention that because it’s been amazing.”
