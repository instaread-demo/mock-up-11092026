Eating Two Cans of Sardines Weekly Dropped Type 2 Diabetes Risk From 37% to 8% in One Year (Fish Oil Pills Didn’t Come Close)

Written by Julien Raby

Last updated on November 8, 2026

A humble tin of sardines may be the most underrated item in your pantry.

Verywell Health recently spotlighted the viral “sardinemaxxing” trend, where fans crack open tins for health optimization.

Behind the memes sits genuine clinical data, including a trial in which older adults with prediabetes saw their risk profile shift dramatically.

Could two cheap tins per week really rival a bottle of fish oil capsules?

Here is what the science supports, and where it still has gaps.

Jump to: [show]

The Prediabetes Trial Behind the Buzz

In a 2021 Clinical Nutrition study led by Díaz-Rizzolo, 152 prediabetic adults over 65 were tracked for a year, with one group eating about 200 grams of sardines weekly (two 100-gram tins).

  • Sardine group: highest-risk share fell from 37% to 8%
  • Control group: it fell from 27% to 22%

Why Caution Still Makes Sense

The study was open-label, so knowing their group may have changed participants' other habits; larger blinded trials are needed.

Whole Fish Versus Capsules: The Food Matrix Argument

After mixed heart results from fish oil trials such as ASCEND, VITAL and STRENGTH, a 2023 Frontiers in Nutrition review by Santos, May and Bueno argued sardines may gain from the food matrix effect, nutrients working better together than alone.

That review is an argument, not a direct comparison trial, so beating fish oil is still a hypothesis.

What One Tin Delivers

  • Omega-3s and taurine: fish oil's EPA and DHA, plus a heart-linked amino acid
  • Complete protein and B12
  • Bone nutrients: 25% to 40% of daily calcium in bone-in tins, varying by brand and portion, with vitamin D3 and phosphorus

A Possible Bonus for Aging Bones

Observational work cited by Eating Well ties weekly sardine eating to a 33% lower hip fracture risk, an association rather than proof of cause.

Low Mercury, Low Cost, Low on the Food Chain

Being low on the food chain, sardines carry far less mercury and PCBs than tuna or swordfish, and the FDA rates them a "Best Choice", including for pregnant women and older adults.

They are also cheap, with MSC-certified tins widely sold.

Why Prediabetes Makes This Timely

The CDC estimates 96 million U.S. adults, about one in three, have prediabetes, most unknowingly.

How to Start Eating Sardines

  • Aim for two 100-gram tins a week, as in the trial, bone-in for calcium
  • Use them on whole-grain toast, in salads or in pasta
  • Check with a healthcare provider first if managing diabetes, on blood thinners or on a medically supervised diet

The Bottom Line

Sardines are cheap, packed with nutrients and low in contaminants; they are not proven better than fish oil, but the article sees replacing processed snacks with them as a low-risk change for most adults.
