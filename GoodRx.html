High-Deductible Health Plans: Pros, Cons, and FAQs

Key takeaways:

  • A high-deductible health plan (HDHP) is health insurance that has a smaller monthly premium and a larger deductible.

  • The federal government defines the deductible amounts for plans to qualify as HDHPs. For 2026, the minimum deductible for an HDHP is $1,700 for individuals and $3,400 for families. In 2027, the minimum deductible is $1,750 for singles and $3,500 for families.

Marketplace premiums rose in 2026 after enhanced ACA tax credits expired, and many people use an HDHP to limit monthly costs.

Its premiums are low and in-network preventive care is usually covered in full, but there is a catch.

Care beyond checkups can get expensive, so it pays to understand the plan first.

What is a high-deductible health plan (HDHP)?

It pairs cheaper premiums with a large deductible, the amount you pay before cost-sharing begins.

The deductible must meet a yearly federal minimum.

How does a high-deductible health plan work?

You pay full price for covered care until the deductible is met, often thousands of dollars.

Costs are then shared until the out-of-pocket maximum, after which covered care is paid in full.

Some plans have separate in- and out-of-network deductibles, so check what counts toward each.

Wellness visits, vaccines and some screenings are usually covered early, which suits healthy, light users.

HDHPs also unlock a tax-advantaged health savings account (HSA).

High-deductible health plans and HSAs

Lower premiums come with a big out-of-pocket bill before coverage kicks in.

An HSA lets you save for medical costs tax-free, and some employers contribute.

Unlike an FSA, the balance rolls over and moves with you between jobs.

What are the 2026 and 2027 IRS limits and rules for an HDHP?

Minimum deductibles are $1,700 (individual) and $3,400 (family) for 2026, and $1,750 and $3,500 for 2027. Out-of-pocket caps, excluding premiums, are $8,500 and $17,000 in 2026, then $8,700 and $17,400.

Past the cap, covered care is free for the rest of the year, except out of network.

What does a high-deductible health plan cover?

In-network preventive care needs no deductible, which can make these plans good value.

Some telehealth may be covered early too, without affecting HSA eligibility.

Advantages of high-deductible health plans

Main benefits:

  • Lower premiums: Cheaper monthly than low-deductible plans.

  • Potential savings: Healthy people needing mostly checkups likely spend less.

  • HSA access: Deposits, growth and qualified withdrawals are all tax-advantaged.

  • Employer benefits: Some employers fund workers’ HSAs.

The One Big Beautiful Bill Act extended HSA eligibility from 2026 by classing ACA bronze and catastrophic plans as HDHPs.

Disadvantages of high-deductible health plan

The downsides:

  • Large medical expenses: An accident or emergency can be very costly.

  • Future health risks: Cost may lead people to delay care, risking worse and pricier problems.

  • High deductibles: Nearly half of adults in a KFF survey find healthcare hard to afford; saved premiums can help.

Is a high deductible or low deductible better?

It depends on your needs: HDHPs suit healthy people who rarely need care, and an HSA adds three tax benefits:

  • Deductible contributions

  • Tax-free growth

  • Tax-free qualified withdrawals

Low-deductible plans cost more monthly but less when you need care.

Expect only preventive care? An HDHP may fit; otherwise higher premiums may be safer.

Frequently asked questions

Who should not use a high-deductible health plan?

People with costly or unpredictable care needs, since nearly everything is paid out of pocket until a deductible that can reach thousands of dollars is met.

Are high-deductible health plans worth it?

Yes, for healthy, light users: preventive care is covered and premiums are low, especially with an HSA.

The bottom line

Lower premiums and HSA access can save healthy people money.

If you need ongoing or unexpected care, you may pay more, so run the numbers for your budget and health.
