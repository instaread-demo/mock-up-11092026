The TSX has enjoyed a successful week so far, reflecting continued investor optimism across several sectors. While artificial intelligence remains one of the market’s most closely watched themes, investors are also paying close attention to developments in defence technology and healthcare innovation. As a result, a number of Canadian companies have attracted significant news flow in recent weeks, giving investors fresh reasons to follow their progress.

Here are three Canadian stocks that have been generating attention lately.

A new identity in Canada’s AI sector

With AI still a leading market theme worldwide, Quanta AI Inc. (TSXV: QNTA, Forum) is drawing more notice.

The firm, previously called Datametrex AI Ltd, adopted its new name after shareholders and regulators signed off, as covered in the rebrand announcement, and its stock has traded as QNTA on the TSX Venture Exchange since September.

One arm, the Korean unit Datametrex Korea, sells enterprise tech such as data-centre and private cloud infrastructure, automation software and managed services to large global clients.

The other arm is a new division that plans to buy, build and license patents, aiming for long-term value through partnerships.

That pairs steady service income with possible patent upside, and the pace of signing licensing deals is likely a key thing to track; the single AI-focused name ties both efforts together and has put the stock back on small-cap watchlists.

Growing exposure to Canada’s defence technology push

Rising government spending on security, surveillance and autonomous systems has made defence a major 2026 investing theme.

MDA Space Ltd. (TSX: MDA, Forum) is riding that wave, as its subsidiary 49North has qualified in every one of the five streams of Canada’s Defence Drone Initiative Marketplace.

That status lets 49North bid on work spanning uncrewed and counter-drone systems, secure communications, testing and training, integration and innovation.

It builds on past drone support work for Canada’s army, navy and air force, plus surveillance and reconnaissance work in tough conditions.

The news highlights MDA’s expansion past space and satellites, and experienced firms could be well placed for future contracts as the initiative pushes homegrown autonomous technology.

Bladder cancer program advances

Clinical news can move biotech stocks quickly, and Theralase Technologies Inc. (TSXV: TLT, Forum) drew interest with an update on its Phase II bladder cancer trial.

The interim results cover light-activated Ruvidar® in BCG-unresponsive non-muscle invasive bladder cancer, with 96 patients enrolled against a target of 90.

About 64.5% of evaluated patients reached a complete response during treatment and 73.1% a complete or indeterminate one, while around two in five responders held a complete response for 15 months, with a median of 14.9 months.

With enrollment closed, Theralase is preparing filings for Health Canada and the U.S. FDA, and expects marketing decisions in 2027 if reviews go to plan.

It also aims to extend into glioblastoma, non-small cell lung cancer and muscle-invasive bladder cancer, and has floated possible antiviral programs.

Those milestones give speculative investors several catalysts to watch over the next year.

The bottom line

Across AI, defence and healthcare, company news brings both opportunity and risk, so investors should look past headlines to each firm’s business, competition, finances and strategy.

Keeping a current watchlist and doing careful research can help portfolios keep pace with change, and coming filings, results and regulatory news will fill in more of each story.
