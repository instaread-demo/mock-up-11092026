Lemon Ginger Turmeric Tea Recipe

Skip the eye-watering shots of apple cider vinegar and start your day with this lemon ginger turmeric tea instead. I make a big batch of this tea concentrate and keep it in the fridge so I can easily mix up a cup whenever I want one. With my kids in school, I know how quickly illnesses spread through a classroom and find their way into our home, so I love having this tea ready to go before I feel a cold or sore throat coming on.

I call this my homemade detox tea recipe, but to be clear, it’s not a turmeric cleanse or meal replacement. It’s a simple drink made with real ingredients that can support hydration and digestion as part of my daily routine. If you’ve wondered how to make detox tea at home, this is the easiest version I know: simmer, strain and keep the concentrate in the fridge.

You’ve probably heard about the benefits of starting your day with lemon water. Lemons contain vitamin C, which supports immune function, and lemon water is an easy way to stay hydrated (Cleveland Clinic). I take that simple habit a step further by adding ginger, turmeric, cinnamon and a little cayenne pepper for extra flavor and to help me feel my best.

Lemon Ginger Turmeric Tea Benefits

This ginger turmeric lemon drink combines several ingredients with potential health benefits. Lemon provides vitamin C to support immune system health. Ginger may help ease nausea and digestive discomfort. Turmeric contains curcumin, a compound studied for its anti-inflammatory effects. Cinnamon adds antioxidants and a warm, comforting flavor.

Each ingredient brings something a little different to the cup, and together they make a warm, caffeine-free drink I actually look forward to. Below, I break down what each one adds, plus tips for using fresh or ground versions depending on what you have in your kitchen.

Keep in mind that this tea is meant to complement balanced meals, regular movement and good sleep, not replace them. I think of it as one small, easy habit that fits into a healthy routine.

Lemon

Lemons are a good source of vitamin C, an antioxidant that protects the body from free radical damage, supports immune health and plays an important role in growing and developing tissues. Vitamin C is also essential for collagen production, which benefits skin, hair and joint health.

Beyond vitamin C, lemons provide small amounts of potassium and B vitamins. Their natural acidity can also support digestion by stimulating stomach acid production, helping the body break down food more effectively.

Tip: Make your lemon easier to squeeze by placing a flat hand firmly on top of it. Then roll it back and forth on the counter with firm pressure. Juice your lemons easily with this lemon squeezer!

Ginger

Ginger is my go-to remedy for nausea, whether from motion sickness, morning sickness or illness, but it can also help with other types of stomach discomfort. Studies show it may help speed up the emptying of food from the stomach into the intestines, which can ease indigestion and bloating. According to the NCCIH, ginger may help with nausea during pregnancy.

Fresh ginger root also has antioxidant, anti-inflammatory and antimicrobial properties that may help reduce muscle soreness and joint pain and support immune function.

When making my ginger detox tea, I like to peel the ginger before slicing it (rather than using grated ginger), but peeling is optional as long as you wash it thoroughly. If you do peel it, a spoon or peeler makes it easy to remove the papery skin. I prefer fresh ginger, but you can use ground ginger as an alternative. Start with 1 teaspoon of ground ginger, then add more to taste.

Turmeric

Turmeric is one of my favorite spices to keep on hand because it’s naturally rich in antioxidants. Its active compound, curcumin, has anti-inflammatory properties that may help reduce inflammation and related discomfort. Adding a little fresh turmeric root to my day is an easy way to give my body some extra support.

You can use turmeric powder instead of fresh turmeric in your tea. Powdered turmeric is more concentrated, so use about 1 teaspoon of ground turmeric for every 1 tablespoon of grated fresh turmeric. Fresh turmeric has a slightly brighter, more vibrant flavor, while powdered turmeric blends more easily. I like to add a dash of black pepper because piperine, a compound in black pepper, helps the body absorb curcumin.

Cinnamon

Cinnamon is a spice packed with antioxidants and potential anti-inflammatory properties. It may also help improve insulin sensitivity, which plays a role in maintaining stable blood sugar levels.

When making this tea, I have used both ground cinnamon and cinnamon sticks, depending on what I have on hand. Cinnamon sticks are great for simmering, while ground cinnamon blends more quickly and evenly into your tea. Either way, you get a warm, comforting flavor.

Cayenne Pepper

Cayenne pepper adds a subtle spicy kick to this lemon ginger turmeric tea, along with some extra health benefits. It contains beta-carotene, which supports immune function, and may also help with circulation and inflammation.

Spiced tea isn’t for everyone, though. I personally skipped cayenne pepper in my hot tea during pregnancy since it gave me heartburn. If you don’t enjoy spicy flavors, feel free to leave it out. For a milder option, try adding a drizzle of raw honey or maple syrup as natural sweeteners instead.

Homemade Lemon Ginger Turmeric Tea Recipe

This homemade lemon ginger turmeric tea combines ginger, turmeric, cinnamon and lemon for a warming, caffeine-free drink. I make the concentrated tea ahead of time and store it in the fridge, then add fresh lemon and hot water whenever I’m ready for a cup.

Ingredients

Scale

For The Tea Concentrate:

  • 6 cups of water
  • 1 chunk fresh ginger (approximately 5-6″ in length), peeled (optional)
  • 3 cinnamon sticks (or 1–2 tsp ground cinnamon)
  • 1 tsp ground turmeric
  • ½ tsp cayenne pepper (optional)
  • Pinch of black pepper (optional)

To Add Fresh Before Serving:

  • Fresh lemons (add ¼-½ lemon, freshly squeezed, per serving)
Cook ModePrevent your screen from going dark

Instructions

  1. Peel the fresh ginger root (optional) and dice it into thin slices.
  2. Combine the fresh ginger slices and water in a large pot or saucepan over high heat on the stove.
  3. Bring to a boil, then turn the heat to low and let it simmer for approximately 10 minutes.
  4. After 10 minutes, add the cinnamon sticks (or ground cinnamon), turmeric, cayenne and black pepper (if using) and simmer on low heat for another 10 minutes, stirring occasionally.
  5. Remove from heat, let cool and strain.
  6. When ready to enjoy, add ¼ cup of the concentrate to 6 oz hot water, then squeeze in the juice of ¼-½ fresh lemon.

Notes

This makes about 4-5 cups of concentrated tea, as some water will steam off while boiling.

I personally like to make this lemon ginger turmeric tea in bulk, so I have the concentrate ready to go in the fridge. Store it in a glass container or large mason jar and enjoy it warm or cold whenever you need an immune booster.

FAQs

When is the best time to drink lemon ginger turmeric tea?

You can drink lemon ginger turmeric tea at any time of day, but I like to drink mine first thing in the morning before a high-fiber breakfast as part of my morning routine. It’s a gentle way to wake up the body and get my digestive system moving.

Is it okay to drink lemon ginger turmeric tea every day?

Yes, most people can enjoy turmeric ginger lemon tea daily in moderation. Drinking 1-2 cups a day is a reasonable guideline that may help boost your immune system, but individual tolerance varies. Drinking too much may cause digestive discomfort, including heartburn, diarrhea or stomach cramps. Turmeric and ginger may also interact with certain medications, so check with a healthcare professional if you take medication or have concerns about drinking this herbal tea.

Does lemon ginger turmeric tea help with weight loss?

No tea burns fat on its own, but this turmeric detox tea is a low-calorie, caffeine-free drink that supports hydration and can serve as a substitute for sugary beverages. For sustainable weight loss and to optimize your health, focus on consistent habits, including regular physical activity and balanced meals with enough protein. Check out my beginner weight loss workout and high-protein meal plan for practical ways to get started.

Which tea is best for a full-body detox?

Personally, I don’t believe there is a single “best” tea for a full-body detox, as detoxification largely relies on the liver, kidneys and overall healthy habits rather than any one beverage. Herbal teas like lemon ginger turmeric, green tea, dandelion and hibiscus can support digestion, hydration and antioxidant intake, which all help the body’s natural detox processes. Choosing a tea you enjoy and drinking it regularly, alongside daily exercise, a balanced diet, proper recovery and electrolytes, is the most effective approach.

How long does a batch of this tea last in the fridge?

This lemon ginger and turmeric tea will stay fresh in the fridge for up to 2 weeks. When storing it, I skip adding the fresh lemon, and instead squeeze in about half a lemon each time I’m ready to enjoy a cup.

Can I drink this ginger tea during pregnancy?

I drank this ginger tea during pregnancy as an alternative to coffee. It helped ease my morning sickness and nausea. I just left out the cayenne pepper and cut back on the turmeric because it gave me heartburn. When I was extra nauseous, I would just add fresh ginger root to warm water.

If you’re pregnant, check with your healthcare provider before drinking this tea, as some studies show turmeric supplements and high doses of turmeric may be unsafe during pregnancy.

Are there any side effects of drinking lemon ginger turmeric tea?

Drinking too much lemon ginger turmeric tea may cause digestive discomfort, including heartburn, diarrhea or stomach cramps. Cayenne and ginger can aggravate heartburn in some people, and turmeric and ginger may interact with certain medications. If you plan to drink this tea daily and take any medication, talk with your healthcare provider first.
